💡 "How Schools Teach It Today" Cheat-Sheets

Ever look at a 1st or 2nd grade worksheet and think, "Why don't they just add it the normal way?" Here are quick 30-second translations so you can feel 100% confident without needing ChatGPT!

1. Number Bonds (Whole – Part – Part)

What it is: Three circles connected by lines. One big circle is the Whole (Total), and two smaller circles are the Parts that add up to it.

( 12 ) ← Whole / \ ( 7 ) ( 5 ) ← Parts (because 7 + 5 = 12, and 12 - 7 = 5)

What to say to the kids: "If the two small circles are friends, they team up to make the big circle! If we know the big circle (12) and one friend (7), count up from 7 to 12 to find the missing friend!"

2. The "Make a 10" Strategy (e.g., 8 + 6)

Why schools teach it: Adding to 10 in your head (10 + 4 = 14) is much easier for an 8-year-old than memorizing 8 + 6.

Problem: 8 + 6 = ? Step 1: How many more does 8 need to become 10? (It needs 2!) Step 2: Break 6 into (2 and 4). Step 3: Give the 2 to the 8 → Now we have 10 + 4 = 14!

Tip: Switch to the 🧮 Math Lab tab at the bottom to show them this live with counters!

3. Subtraction with "Regrouping" (Borrowing)

What changed: Schools now call "borrowing" Regrouping or Unbundling a Ten so kids understand *why* a little "1" appears next to the ones digit.

Problem: 42 − 17 "More on the floor? Go next door and get 10 more!" • We have 2 Ones, so we can't take away 7 Ones yet. • Trade 1 of our 4 Tens for 10 Ones. • Now we have 3 Tens and 12 Ones! • 12 − 7 = 5 Ones, and 3 − 1 = 2 Tens → Answer: 25!

4. Tape Diagrams / Bar Models (Word Problems)

What it is: Drawing a rectangular bar split into boxes to see if a word problem is asking for the Total (Add) or a Missing Part (Subtract).

[ 8 Red Apples | ? Green Apples ] [ 15 Apples in All ] → Since 15 is the whole bar, we subtract: 15 − 8 = 7!

5. Phonics: "Magic E" & "Two Vowels Go Walking"

• Bossy / Magic E: An silent e at the end of a word jumps back and makes the vowel say its own alphabet name (cap → cape, kit → kite, hop → hope).
• Vowel Teams (ea, oa, ai, ee): "When two vowels go walking, the first one does the talking (says its name)!" — like boat (says O), rain (says A), team (says E).
